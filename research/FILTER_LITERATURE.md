# Exact quantized recursive filtering: literature comparison

Reviewed 4 November 2026. Scope: the scalar transition `clip(floor((A*s+C)/D))`, with `0 <= A < D`, wide intermediate arithmetic, and state-independent known inputs/coefficient schedules. Project inputs read: `SPEC.md` and the supplied evidence appendix. This is a targeted primary-source review, not a priority or patent opinion. No hardware experiments were performed.

## Finding

Parallel recursive filtering, block boundary propagation, exact function scans, saturation-preserving associative summaries, and speculative finite-state execution are established. The defensible research question is narrower: **whether reverse integer threshold construction makes exact block maps of contractive, rounded scalar recurrences cheap enough to outperform competing exact implementations at an application-acceptable delay**. The sources inspected do not establish that this particular construction was published; they also do not establish its novelty. The most important unresolved classic comparison is quantizer-loop pipelining, not ordinary linear look-ahead alone.

In particular, do not describe the work as the first parallel IIR, first nonlinear recursive scan, first exact saturation-preserving parallelization, or first finite-precision DSP analysis. Separate mathematical representation, efficient construction, and physical hardware advantage into three claims with separate evidence.

## Reading depth and source-specific findings

**[1] K. K. Parhi and D. G. Messerschmitt. “Pipeline Interleaving and Parallelism in Recursive Digital Filters. I. Pipelining Using Scattered Look-Ahead and Decomposition.” IEEE TASSP 37(7), 1099–1117, 1989.** [Author bibliography](https://people.eecs.berkeley.edu/~messer/Publications.htm). Depth: author bibliographic confirmation; the journal full text was not obtained. Do not characterize its roundoff derivation as newly audited here. The supplied appendix records an earlier selected-page review, which is a project record, not this review's direct reading.

**[2] Same authors. Part II: “Pipelined Incremental Block Filtering.” IEEE TASSP 37(7), 1118–1134, 1989; DOI 10.1109/29.32287.** [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1109/29.32287), [author thesis/report PDF](https://digicoll.lib.berkeley.edu/record/136130/files/ERL-88-61.pdf). Depth: bibliographic verification and indexed thesis chapter extract only; PDF access returned 403. The extract describes combining clustered/scattered look-ahead, decomposition and incremental output computation, with linear multiplication complexity in block size and logarithmic complexity in pipeline stages. This is not sufficient to verify equivalence of every quantizer placement. **Open reading obligation:** obtain journal Part II and its roundoff appendix, identify every quantizer location, distinguish noise/statistical equality from sequence equality, and reproduce an example under the project's signed floor/saturation rule. The requested full classic comparison remains incomplete until then.

**[3] K. K. Parhi. “Finite Word Effects in Pipelined Recursive Filters.” IEEE TSP 39(6), 1450–1454, 1991; DOI 10.1109/78.136557.** [Institutional abstract and metadata](https://experts.umn.edu/en/publications/finite-word-effects-in-pipelined-recursive-filters/). Depth: abstract only. It explicitly treats inexact pole-zero cancellation and both coefficient quantization and roundoff. Its objective is maintaining output error with increased precision, not a stated identity to one original rounded state trajectory. Obtain the full correspondence before using its quantitative precision bound.

**[4] K. K. Parhi. “Pipelining in Algorithms with Quantizer Loops.” IEEE TCAS 38(7), 745–754, 1991; DOI 10.1109/31.135746.** [Author publication list](https://ece.umn.edu/users/parhi/SLIDES/Parhi_Publications_2015.pdf), [author research description](https://www.ee.umn.edu/users/parhi/research/highlights/). Depth: author metadata and technique description only, not full method. This is a high-priority unresolved predecessor. A claim that “look-ahead cannot handle quantization” would be untenable without qualification. Compare exact branches versus relaxed/approximate look-ahead separately; these are different contracts.

**[5] D. Nehab, A. Maximo, R. S. Lima and H. Hoppe. “GPU-Efficient Recursive Filtering and Summed-Area Tables.” ACM TOG / SIGGRAPH Asia, 2011.** [Author paper](https://hhoppe.com/recursive.pdf), [project/code/benchmarks](https://w3.impa.br/~diego/projects/NehEtAl11/). Depth: full paper available; inspected formulation, block decomposition and conclusions/evaluation, not reproduced. Boundary bands accumulate successive linear filters and reduce global-memory passes. This establishes block-parallel recursive image processing and cross-filter locality, but does not supply the project's inverse integer threshold representation. Its multiple dimensions and higher-order filters make it broader in another direction; do not present scalar thresholds as a general replacement.

**[6] G. Chaurasia, J. Ragan-Kelley, S. Paris, G. Drettakis and F. Durand. “Compiling High Performance Recursive Filters.” HPG 2015; DOI 10.1145/2790060.2790063.** [Full author paper](https://mit-gfx.github.io/recfilter/recfilter.pdf), [supplement](https://mit-gfx.github.io/recfilter/supplement.pdf). Depth: inspected specification and transformations, particularly Sections 4–6; evaluation surveyed, compiler not run. Section 6 uses independent intra-tile scans, extracts tails, completes them by a serial inter-tile matrix recurrence, then recomputes intra-tile results. These are linear-superposition transformations, including reordering/cascading. No proof of identical per-step floor/saturation behavior or reverse threshold construction was found. Architectural overlap is substantial: local work, boundary reconciliation, local recomputation. The distinction must be the exact nonlinear summary and cost, not that schedule alone.

**[7] K. Ma, A. Adams and J. Ragan-Kelley. “Finding Fast Filters.” arXiv:2607.20634v1, submitted 22 July 2026.** [Full HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2607.20634v1), [version metadata](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.20634). Depth: inspected representation, fitting/lowering and evaluation sections, not run. It searches approximate filters and reports a performance/quality frontier. Section 5 uses float32 and expressly omits quantization. That excludes direct anticipation of an exact quantized executor in the inspected version; it does not by itself establish a new compiler contribution. Treat as a preprint unless a final venue is separately verified.

**[8] K. Papadantonakis, N. Kapre, S. Chan and A. DeHon. “Pipelining Saturated Accumulation.” FPT 2005.** [Author extended paper](https://ic.ese.upenn.edu/pdf/sataccum_icfpt2005_extended.pdf). Depth: inspected Sections 2–3, closure/composition construction; deeper hardware audit belongs in the companion prior-art review. Saturating translation functions close under a three-parameter representation and compose associatively, enabling reduction/prefix trees while preserving saturation order. This is particularly strong prior art because it already targets FPGA recursive bottlenecks with exact nonlinear function composition. Its unity-slope translations do not require the project's quantized contraction staircase, but the generic summary/scan rationale is shared.

**[9] T. Mytkowicz, M. Musuvathi and W. Schulte. “Data-Parallel Finite-State Machines.” ASPLOS 2014.** [Microsoft paper and metadata](https://www.microsoft.com/en-us/research/publication/data-parallel-finite-state-machines/), [full PDF](https://www.microsoft.com/en-us/research/wp-content/uploads/2016/02/asplos302-mytkowicz.pdf). Depth: abstract and method overview checked in this pass; the appendix's deeper partial-convergence reading was not independently repeated. Exact finite-state enumeration is established. An honest comparison measures all-state tables, image-set/convergence reductions and restricted forward enumeration against threshold construction, rather than contrasting only with naive serial execution.

**[10] A. Cox, S. Sankaranarayanan and B.-Y. E. Chang. “A Bit Too Precise? Verification of Quantized Digital Filters.” STTT 16(2), 175–190, 2014.** [Author institutional publication record](https://plv.colorado.edu/bec/papers/filters-sttt13.html), [author manuscript](https://arlencox.github.io/papers/filters.pdf). Depth: inspected arithmetic/problem framing and verification approach, not tool replication. This corrects the appendix's unresolved date: the author's record identifies the journal publication as 2014; distinguish it from the 2012 TACAS bounded-verification precursor. It considers roundoff, overflow and limit cycles using bit reasoning and real-error approximations. It is a correctness comparator, not a claim of this threshold executor.

**[11] Arm CMSIS-DSP, “Biquad Cascade IIR Filters Using Direct Form I Structure.”** [Official documentation](https://arm-software.github.io/CMSIS-DSP/latest/group__BiquadCascadeDF1.html). Depth: fixed-point and individual variant arithmetic inspected. Fast Q15 can wrap its accumulator; standard Q15 uses wider accumulation. Fast Q31 truncates products differently from standard Q31. A first-order specialization is possible, but validate its exact instruction semantics against the project before labeling it a bit-exact baseline. Pin library version/commit and target ISA in experiments.

**[12] AMD, Versal DSP Engine Architecture Manual AM004.** [Official architecture documentation](https://docs.amd.com/r/en-US/am004-versal-dsp-engine/DSP58-Architecture). Depth: architecture/resource section inventory only, no implementation-specific timing audit. The manual distinguishes rounding/saturation, division and pipeline design considerations. Do not count an inverse ceiling division, comparator network or threshold-memory access as one multiply-accumulate cycle without implementation evidence.

**[13] H. Zhai and B.-P. Paris. “Parallel Cascaded Recursive Filtering on Multi-Core CPUs and GPUs.” arXiv:2607.23763v1, 26 July 2026.** [Full paper](https://arxiv.org/html/2607.23763v1). Depth: arithmetic background, state propagation, CPU granularity and GPU mapping/cost sections inspected; not reproduced. The method splits zero-state responses and homogeneous corrections by superposition, then uses CPU wavefront execution or GPU decoupled lookback. Sections III–IV specify single precision and four-byte samples, not the project's integer floor/saturation semantics. Its [companion block-matrix paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.14054) is identified but not independently read here. Add both to the numerical throughput baseline shortlist; do not compare headline batch rates with small-block exact streaming without matching delay and order. The work also points to Maleki–Burtscher's ASPLOS 2018 linear-recurrence compiler as a further baseline obligation.

**[14] S. Maleki and M. Burtscher. “Automatic Hierarchical Parallelization of Linear Recurrences.” ASPLOS 2018; DOI 10.1145/3173162.3173168.** [Author full paper](https://userweb.cs.txstate.edu/~burtscher/papers/asplos18.pdf), [PLR code generator](https://userweb.cs.txstate.edu/~burtscher/research/PLR/). Depth: inspected recurrence specification, two-phase merging/correction method and Sections 5–6 arithmetic/validation. It supports constant-coefficient linear recurrences; adjacent chunks are merged using precomputed correction factors before pipelined completion. Integer experiments use integer coefficients and 32-bit sequences, with exact-match validation. Digital-filter experiments use float32, accepting differences within `10^-3` because reassociation changes floating-point results. Therefore “prior compilers are never exact” is false, but exactness of integer linear arithmetic does not extend to a fractional coefficient followed by floor and clipping at every step. Benchmark PLR for compatible numerical/application contracts and use its hierarchy as a scheduling comparator, not as an already-correct executor of the project's nonlinear machine.

## Exclusions and experiment obligations

The project proof does not cover general biquads, negative-feedback scalar maps, arbitrary nonlinear DSP, multidimensional states, floating-point exceptional values, wraparound, state-dependent coefficient adaptation, or unspecified truncation. Cascading scalar sections may retain local applicability, but creates another scheduling/resource problem and does not grant a scalar threshold representation to the combined state.

An affine scan over real/rational coefficients preserves ideal algebra, not generally per-step integer rounding. A bounded-error implementation may be a useful application competitor but belongs on a separate exactness/quality axis. Coalescence is not guaranteed: the appendix's `3/4` silence example retains multiple fixed states. Therefore `L ≈ tau*ln(R/K)` in `SPEC.md` is a heuristic sizing relation, not a worst-case capacity or speedup theorem.

Required exact baselines: (a) best serial RTL with constant-coefficient shift/add specialization and sufficient internal precision; (b) replication/interleaving across genuinely independent channels, reporting per-channel delay separately; (c) full-table transfer composition at small widths; (d) forward-only restricted tables/candidate replay; (e) threshold maps with both serial boundary propagation and tree resolution; (f) quantizer-branch/delayed-decision implementation once [4] is obtained. Include unity-slope saturated accumulation as a separate sanity/control case, where [8]'s constant-size summary should win over generic staircase storage.

Measure routed frequency, sustained samples/second, first and worst-case output delay, LUT/FF/DSP/BRAM, divider implementations, buffer traffic, threshold distribution and certified maximum K, fallback frequency and worst-case fallback deadline, plus energy only if measured. Sweep precision, coefficient distance from one, block size and channel count, including saturation-heavy, silence, adversarial and native traces. Normalize at both equal device resources and equal throughput/latency targets. The appendix's `(K+3)L` counts heterogeneous transitions; it is not cycle cost, and `P/W` alone is not a system speedup model.

## Credible publication positioning

A cautious candidate claim is: “an exact, order-aware block-summary construction for a restricted family of quantized contractive recurrences, with measured throughput/latency/resource tradeoffs.” Publication readiness requires closing [2]/[4], proving signed division/sentinel/width invariants, and showing a useful operating region against the exact baselines. A representation theorem without implementation evidence supports an algorithmic note, not a hardware superiority claim.

If hardware evidence succeeds, **SiPS** is a natural systems audience: its [official 2026 call](https://ieee-cas.org/files/ieeecass/2025-12/sips-2026-cfp.pdf) includes signal-processing design/implementation and FPGA/reconfigurable systems. **ACM FPGA** becomes credible with a stronger architecture/tool contribution and convincing comparisons: its [official 2026 scope](https://isfpga.org/past/fpga2026/call-for-papers/) includes high-level tools and applications/design studies. These are audience-fit observations, not acceptance predictions or available 2026 submission windows. A compiler venue needs reusable transformations and meaningful language coverage beyond one specialized recurrence; an exactness distinction alone is insufficient.
